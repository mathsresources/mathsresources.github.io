Junior Maths Challenge Rooms

Solve each problem to unlock the next room.
Enter numerical answers only. Fractions such as 1/4 are accepted.

Room 1

Four of the following numbers are prime. Enter the number which is not prime.

\[ 101,\quad 103,\quad 107,\quad 109,\quad 111 \]

Room 2

Calculate the value of the following expression.

\[ 2020 \div 20 \]

Room 3

How many centimetres are equal to \(66.6\) metres?

\[ 66.6\text{ m} = \ ?\text{ cm} \]

Room 4

Amrita chooses a number. She doubles it, adds \(9\), divides the result by \(3\), and then subtracts \(1\). Her final result is the number she chose originally. What was her number?

\[ \frac{2x+9}{3}-1=x \]

Room 5

Calculate the value of the following expression. Give your answer as a fraction or decimal.

\[ \frac{6}{12} -\frac{5}{12} +\frac{4}{12} -\frac{3}{12} +\frac{2}{12} -\frac{1}{12} \]

Room 6

Four different positive integers have a product of \(110\). What is their sum?

\[ a \times b \times c \times d=110 \]

Room 7

One half of one third of one quarter of one fifth of a number is \(2\). What is the number?

\[ \frac{1}{2} \times\frac{1}{3} \times\frac{1}{4} \times\frac{1}{5} \times x =2 \]

Final Room

How many of these four equations are satisfied when \(x=12\)?

\[ x-2=10 \] \[ \frac{x}{2}=24 \] \[ 10-x=2 \] \[ 2x-1=25 \]
